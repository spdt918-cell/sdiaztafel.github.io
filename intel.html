Intel — Sustainability Impact Analysis

Used SQL to analyze 601,740 repurposed devices from Intel's 2024 sustainability program — finding where the program was leaving environmental impact on the table, and building a data-driven recommendation to fix it.

The Task

Intel's repurposing program extends the life of laptops and desktops instead of manufacturing new ones — reducing e-waste, energy consumption, and CO₂ emissions. The question was: is the program targeting the right devices, in the right regions, to maximize environmental impact?

I was given two datasets — device data and impact data — and asked to join, clean, bucket, and analyze them to surface patterns and build a concrete recommendation. Everything was done in SQL.

The Approach

The analysis ran in four stages. First, I joined the two tables on device_id and calculated a device_age column. Then I used a CASE WHEN clause to bucket devices into newer (0–3 years), mid-age (4–6 years), and older (7+ years) groups. From there I aggregated by device type, age bucket, and region to find where the energy savings and CO₂ reductions were actually coming from. The final query used multiple CTEs to calculate each device type's percentage contribution to regional totals.

Here's the core bucketing query that drove most of the analysis:

SELECT
  d.*,
  i.*,
  (2024 - d.model_year) AS device_age,
  CASE
    WHEN (2024 - d.model_year) <= 3  THEN 'newer'
    WHEN (2024 - d.model_year) <= 6  THEN 'mid-age'
    WHEN (2024 - d.model_year) >  6  THEN 'older'
  END AS device_age_bucket
FROM intel.device_data d
INNER JOIN intel.impact_data i
  ON d.device_id = i.device_id

Key Findings

Finding 01

Volume vs. impact tradeoff

The program mostly repurposes newer devices (317,191) but older devices save 48 kWh per unit vs. 19 kWh for newer ones — more than double the per-unit impact. The program is prioritizing volume over environmental return.

Finding 02

Laptops drive total savings

Laptops account for 408,064 of 601,740 devices — more than twice as many as desktops. Since average savings per device are nearly identical, laptop volume is what moves the needle on total CO₂ saved.

Finding 03

Region changes everything

Energy savings per device are nearly equal across regions (~25.7 kWh), but Asia saves 0.0155 tons of CO₂ per device vs. 0.0064 tons in Europe — because Asia's grid relies heavily on coal, so every kWh saved prevents far more emissions.

Finding 04

Scale of the program

601,740 devices at ~25.7 kWh each adds up to ~15.5 million kWh saved and 6,678 tons of CO₂ — roughly equivalent to taking 1,500 cars off the road for a year.

The Recommendation

Route older laptops to Asia.

Older devices save 48 kWh per unit vs. 19 kWh for newer ones, and laptops already make up the majority of repurposed devices. Asia's fossil-fuel-heavy grid means each device saves 0.0155 tons of CO₂ — more than double Europe's 0.0064 tons. Combining the highest per-unit savings with the highest-impact region creates the best possible environmental return per device.

The constraint: only 20,239 older devices were repurposed in 2024, so Intel would also need to expand collection partnerships to source more of them. And while transportation costs to Asia could offset some gains, that analysis would require cost data not available in this dataset.

What I Took Away

The most interesting part wasn't the SQL — it was the moment the two findings connected. I had older devices saving more energy per unit in one query, and Asia preventing more CO₂ per kWh in another. When I asked why Asia's numbers were so different, the answer was coal. That's when the recommendation wrote itself.

It's a good reminder that data analysis isn't just about running queries — it's about knowing when two separate findings are actually one bigger insight.
