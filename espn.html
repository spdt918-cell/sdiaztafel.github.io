"Know The Feeling" — ESPN Campaign

A social-first marketing campaign designed to bring younger, casual fans into ESPN's prime time NFL broadcasts — by selling the feeling of the game instead of the rules.

The Problem

ESPN launched prime time NFL broadcasts but faced a real audience gap — younger viewers weren't showing up. Traditional sports marketing was missing them entirely, leaning on legacy, rules, and team knowledge to sell a product that Gen Z wasn't buying.

The data backed this up: NFL viewership has been declining in younger generations, and ESPN's prime time numbers dropped 5% from 2022–2023 among viewers under 30. Meanwhile, social media accounts for 62% of NFL engagement for fans under 30 — a channel traditional campaigns were barely using.

The Strategy

The insight behind "Know The Feeling" is simple: nearly 70% of fans say they watch sports for entertainment rather than technical knowledge. So instead of teaching people football, the campaign meets them where they already are — emotionally.

The approach focused on three pillars. First, platform-first content — short vertical clips built for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts that look like fan footage, not polished ads. Second, creator partnerships — working with micro-influencers (50k–500k followers), meme pages, and vloggers known for humor and reactions rather than sports commentary. Third, interactivity — live polls, hashtag campaigns, and communal watch parties to make viewers feel part of something, even from home.

The target audience — young adults who stream everything, scroll constantly, and watch sports socially rather than out of loyalty — is under-targeted by traditional sports marketing. That's the opening.

Creative Concepts

All content leans into vertical, raw, phone-shot footage — authenticity over polish. Two hashtag concepts anchor the campaign:

#TheBallIsEverything

Treats football like a religion. Dramatic fan voiceovers, people dropping to their knees over big plays, viral moments that turn the sport into something bigger than a game. Pulls in hardcore fans and people who just want to be part of something.

#BettingMyFeelingsAway

Captures the chaos younger fans who sports bet actually feel. Fans flipping out over missed parlays, creators making skits about bad bets. Taps into a cultural moment that traditional sports marketing completely ignores.

The Takeaway

The goal wasn't to teach new viewers football — it was to give them a reason to care. By focusing on emotion over tradition and creators over TV spots, "Know The Feeling" meets a younger audience on their terms and gives them a reason to show up.

If it works, it doesn't just become a campaign — it becomes a movement.
